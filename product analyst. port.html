About me

I'm a product analyst who cares about the "why" behind the numbers.

My work starts with a business question, not a dashboard. I map how users move through a product, find where they get stuck, and turn that into recommendations a team can act on, often in the form of an experiment.

I'm especially careful with data quality. On live product data, I've caught duplicate tracking events, events that fire automatically, and screens that only some users see, all of which would have quietly distorted the results.

I work mainly with SQL, Python, and PostHog, and I write up my analysis so that anyone on the team can follow the reasoning.

How I work

Every project follows the same path, from a clear question to a decision.

Frame the question

Start from the decision the team needs to make, and define the metric that answers it.

Check the data

Verify tracking, time windows, and definitions before trusting any number.

Analyze

Funnels, cohorts, and statistical tests to find what's happening and how confident we can be.

Recommend and test

Turn findings into a clear recommendation, and design an experiment to prove it.

Fixing onboarding drop-off in an AI audio storytelling app

Where do new users drop off between installing the app and their first listen, and what should the team test first?

5.7%of installers reached a first listen
24%lost at the genres screen
Read the case study

The problem

Installs grew six-fold in three months, but the share of new users reaching their first story kept falling. The team needed to know where users were getting lost.

What I did

  • Explored more than 200 tracked events and used path analysis to reconstruct the onboarding flow, which revealed separate listener and creator paths.
  • Built an install-to-first-listen funnel and a screen-by-screen onboarding funnel for July to September 2026.
  • Fixed several data issues first, including two competing install events and a "first listen" event that fired automatically.

What I found

64% of installers never started onboarding, and 24% of users who reached the genres screen quit there. It also took four times longer than the previous screen.

Recommendation

An A/B test making genre selection optional, configured in PostHog, with day-7 retention and recommendation clicks as guardrails and a 5-week run sized to detect a 5-point lift.

Repository is private; a full walkthrough is available on request.

Where should a mobile game place its first gate?

Does moving Cookie Cats' first gate from level 30 to level 40 change player retention? Analysis of 90,189 players.

−0.8 pts7-day retention with the later gate
p ≈ 0.002statistically significant
Read the case study

What I did

  • Ran sanity checks, including a sample ratio mismatch test, and removed an extreme outlier.
  • Compared day-1 and day-7 retention with two-proportion z-tests and a 1,000-sample bootstrap.
  • Checked engagement with a Mann-Whitney U test, since game rounds were heavily skewed.

What I found

The later gate lowered 7-day retention from 19.0% to 18.2%, with gate 30 ahead in about 99% of bootstrap samples. The sample ratio check flagged a small imbalance, which I sized and addressed rather than ignored.

Recommendation

Keep the gate at level 30, verify the assignment logic, and test dynamic gates that give fast-progressing players an earlier break.
